    →Predictive↻Adaptive⇄Hybrid
    Predictive treatment
    Establishes the relevant plan or definition with greater upfront detail and controlled updates.
    Adaptive equivalent or treatment
    The management purpose is handled iteratively through vision, roadmap, backlog refinement, iteration or release planning, feedback, and progressive elaboration.
    Hybrid treatment
    Adaptive planning cadence is coordinated with predictive milestones, funding, compliance, contracts, or approved control points.

    The registered adaptive source explicitly supports iterative planning, refinement, forecasting, feedback, and hybrid governance overlays.

    Predictive, adaptive, and hybrid treatment
    Predictive
    The project manager coordinates the formally named process with functional, specialist, governance, and acceptance roles acting within documented thresholds.
    Adaptive
    The Product Owner orders value decisions, Developers or the delivery team perform the work, and the Scrum Master enables Scrum effectiveness; specialist and organizational authorities remain explicit.
    Hybrid
    Delivery-level decisions stay close to the team while the project manager connects evidence and exceptions to predictive governance, funding, contract, compliance, and acceptance rights.

    Scope: Typical allocation only; the charter, agreements, policy, and team design determine the actual assignment and delegation.

    Purpose

    Develop a detailed description of the project and product boundaries by selecting and clarifying the work and results the project will include.

    Why This Process Exists

    A requirements set can contain competing wishes, optional features, and unresolved boundaries. Scope definition turns that information into a coherent statement of what the project will deliver and what lies outside its responsibility. Weak boundaries create expectation gaps and uncontrolled expansion.

    How It Fits in the Sequence

    Defined requirements, assumptions, constraints, and planning decisions provide the basis for the detailed scope. The result supports decomposition in Create WBS, schedule and cost planning, quality criteria, and later scope validation and control. It may be refined iteratively as product understanding improves through approved decisions.

    Common Confusions

    Collect Requirements identifies and analyzes needs; Define Scope chooses and describes the included boundary; Create WBS decomposes that approved boundary into manageable components. Definition is not decomposition.

    Practical Example

    PRACTICAL EXAMPLE

    A museum plans a traveling exhibition. Requirements include multilingual interpretation, educational programming, insurance, and possible augmented reality. The team defines the funded exhibition boundary, explicitly excludes a permanent gallery renovation, and passes the approved scope description into WBS development and estimating.

    Exam Tips

    EXAM TIP
    1. Look for decisions about what is included and excluded, not merely a list of stakeholder requests.
    2. The detailed scope description precedes decomposition into the WBS.
    3. Refining scope is legitimate; changing an approved baseline later requires appropriate control.
