    Delivery approach applicability

    →Predictive↻Adaptive⇄Hybrid
    Predictive treatment
    Uses planned communication and engagement activities with documented responsibilities and reporting paths.
    Adaptive equivalent or treatment
    Uses frequent collaboration, visible work, reviews, feedback, and direct stakeholder or team interaction.
    Hybrid treatment
    Combines adaptive collaboration cadence with formal reporting, authority, escalation, and governance needs.
    Why these badges
    The registered adaptive source explicitly retains stakeholder engagement and team coordination while tailoring cadence and artifacts.

    Source-limited crosswalk: Predictive classification is supported by the canonical process catalog. Cross-approach treatments are an Atlas synthesis of the registered 2026 course snapshot and are not a PMI process-to-agile-framework crosswalk.

    Purpose

    Improve team members’ capabilities, working relationships, and environment so the project team can perform effectively together.

    Why This Process Exists

    Assembling skilled individuals does not automatically create a capable project team. Shared practices, trust, feedback, learning, and a workable environment reduce coordination loss and improve collective performance. Development is especially important when the team is new, distributed, or working across organizational boundaries.

    How It Fits in the Sequence

    Acquired team members and the resource-management approach establish the starting conditions. Development activities strengthen capability during execution and can respond to observations from Manage Team; learning also supports project knowledge. Team development continues as membership and challenges change.

    Common Confusions

    Develop Team builds capability and cohesion. Manage Team tracks performance, gives feedback, resolves conflict, and coordinates changes for specific performance needs. Acquire Resources obtains the people in the first place.

    Practical Example

    PRACTICAL EXAMPLE

    A distributed product-launch team has marketing, regulatory, engineering, and regional sales members who have never worked together. They establish shared decision routines, practice handoffs through a launch simulation, and pair regional staff with subject experts. Later performance issues are handled through Manage Team.

    Exam Tips

    EXAM TIP
    1. Training, team-building, recognition, and improved working conditions point toward team development.
    2. Conflict can reveal a development need, but handling a specific performance issue is often Manage Team.
    3. Team development is iterative; it does not end after a kickoff workshop.
