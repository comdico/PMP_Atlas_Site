    Adaptive and Scrum Accountabilities

    The Scrum accountabilities

    The Scrum Team consists of one Product Owner, one Scrum Master, and Developers. These are accountabilities defined by the Scrum Guide, not a complete organizational chart.

    The Product Owner is accountable for maximizing the value of the product and for effective Product Backlog management. That includes communicating the Product Goal, creating and ordering Product Backlog items, and ensuring the backlog is transparent and understood.

    Developers are accountable for creating a usable increment each Sprint. They plan the Sprint Backlog, build quality through the Definition of Done, adapt their plan each day, and hold one another accountable as professionals.

    The Scrum Master is accountable for establishing Scrum as defined in the Scrum Guide and for the Scrum Team’s effectiveness. The Scrum Master coaches, removes or helps remove impediments, and supports effective events and organizational understanding. The role is not simply a meeting scheduler or administrative assistant.

    How these roles differ from project roles

    The Product Owner is not automatically the project sponsor, customer, project manager, procurement authority, compliance authority, or benefit owner. Developers are not necessarily every specialist needed by the organization. The Scrum Master does not direct the team as a traditional line manager.

    A project manager may still coordinate funding, vendors, cross-team dependencies, organizational reporting, risks, transition, and governance when adaptive delivery occurs inside a project. The arrangement should avoid two people independently ordering the same work or making conflicting commitments.

    For ServiceBridge, the Product Owner orders portal capabilities and clarifies desired outcomes. Developers create usable increments. The Scrum Master improves team effectiveness. The project manager coordinates the seller agreement, integrated milestone, compliance evidence, organizational risks, and transition. The sponsor retains the project-level authorization and funding route.

    Authority remains contextual

    Scrum defines product and team accountabilities; it does not grant universal organizational authority. Contracts, finance, compliance, personnel decisions, formal customer acceptance, and project closure follow the organization’s actual governance.

    In a purely product-operating context, there may be no project manager. In a hybrid project, Scrum accountabilities can operate inside project governance. Titles may be combined only when the responsibilities remain clear and conflicts are managed.

    On the exam, do not assign work to Developers as if they lack self-management, do not treat the Scrum Master as the team’s boss, and do not assume the Product Owner owns every project or organizational decision.

    Scrum defines three specific accountabilities. Kanban, by contrast, starts with the current way of working and does not prescribe a replacement role set.

    Scrum accountability model

    The Product Owner is accountable for maximizing product value and effective Product Backlog management. The Scrum Master is accountable for establishing Scrum and Scrum Team effectiveness. Developers are accountable for creating a usable Increment each Sprint. The entire Scrum Team is accountable for creating a valuable, useful Increment.

    Do not translate these into project-manager, team-manager, or approval roles by title alone.

    Product Owner

    Maximizes product value and manages the Product Backlog effectively.

    Responsibilities

    • Develop and communicate the Product Goal
    • Order Product Backlog items
    • Ensure backlog transparency

    Accountabilities

    • Maximizing product value
    • Effective Product Backlog management
    Authority
    Makes Product Backlog ordering decisions; the organization respects those decisions.
    Boundary
    One accountable person in Scrum, not a committee; may delegate work but remains accountable and is not automatically the project sponsor or acceptance authority.
    Delivery context
    A Scrum accountability; hybrid governance must state how backlog authority connects to baselines, funding, contracts, and acceptance.

    Scrum Master

    Establishes Scrum and enables the Scrum Team to improve its effectiveness.

    Responsibilities

    • Coach self-management
    • Help remove impediments
    • Ensure Scrum events are productive

    Accountabilities

    • Establishing Scrum
    • Scrum Team effectiveness
    Authority
    Facilitates and coaches Scrum use; does not assign work or own product decisions by virtue of the accountability.
    Boundary
    Not the team manager, project manager, or Product Owner solely because of this accountability.
    Delivery context
    A Scrum accountability; in hybrid work it may coexist with project integration and governance roles.

    Developers

    Create a usable Increment each Sprint.

    Responsibilities

    • Create the Sprint Backlog
    • Adhere to the Definition of Done
    • Adapt the plan daily

    Accountabilities

    • Usable Increment
    • Sprint plan
    • Quality
    • Mutual professional accountability
    Authority
    Self-manage how Sprint work is performed within the Product Goal, Sprint Goal, and Definition of Done.
    Boundary
    The term is not limited to software developers and does not confer sponsor, product, or acceptance authority.
    Delivery context
    A Scrum accountability held by the people creating the Increment.

    Issue or Impediment Owner

    Drives resolution of a current problem or blocker.

    Responsibilities

    • Define actions
    • Track progress
    • Escalate stalled or out-of-authority resolution

    Accountabilities

    • Visible status
    • Timely action
    • Escalation
    Authority
    Coordinates resolution within assigned limits.
    Boundary
    Assignment does not confer authority to alter controlled commitments.
    Delivery context
    In Scrum, impediment removal may be enabled by the Scrum Master while ownership remains with the person able to resolve it.

    Kanban Service Team

    Manages and improves the flow of work through an existing service or workflow.

    Responsibilities

    • Make work visible
    • Manage flow
    • Improve collaboratively

    Accountabilities

    • Flow transparency
    • Service improvement
    Authority
    Uses existing organizational decision rights unless explicit policies delegate additional flow decisions.
    Boundary
    Kanban does not require replacing existing roles or titles with a prescribed accountability set.
    Delivery context
    Kanban is applied to an existing way of working and can coexist with predictive, adaptive, or hybrid governance.
