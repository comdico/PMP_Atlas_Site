    Predictive Responsibilities

    Predictive roles coordinate through defined responsibility and authority

    Predictive delivery commonly assigns responsibility through approved plans, organizational roles, work packages, and governance thresholds. The purpose is not to create a rigid hierarchy; it is to make clear who performs work, who integrates it, who supplies specialist judgment, and who can authorize a commitment or exception.

    The project manager coordinates the integrated project but does not automatically own every technical, commercial, functional, compliance, or acceptance decision. Team members perform and estimate work. Functional managers may supply people and retain personnel authority. Subject-matter experts provide specialized analysis. Customers or designated representatives decide acceptance. Sponsors and governance bodies act on reserved funding, priority, or closure matters. A responsibility assignment matrix can show participation, but actual authority also comes from the charter, policies, agreements, and organizational design.

    For ServiceBridge, the project manager is responsible for presenting a recovery analysis after a seller delay. The scheduler updates the network, the procurement authority assesses contract rights, the quality lead protects required evidence, and the sponsor decides whether additional funding is authorized. Assigning the project manager responsibility for coordination does not give that role authority to alter the seller agreement or approve more funding. Hybrid projects retain these boundaries while adaptive teams decide product and technical matters within delegated limits. On the exam, separate the person who analyzes or recommends from the person whose documented authority approves, accepts, or authorizes.

    Predictive delivery commonly names a project manager, project team, functional managers, specialists, and governance authorities. The exact allocation remains tailored.

    Responsibility pattern

    | Moment | Typical lead | Critical boundary |

    | --- | --- | --- |

    | Authorize | Sponsor | Authorization is not detailed planning. |

    | Plan and integrate | Project manager | Integration does not absorb specialist authority. |

    | Perform | Project team and functional resources | Performing work does not grant approval authority. |

    | Verify | Quality or specialist authority | Verification is not acceptance. |

    | Accept | Customer or acceptance authority | Acceptance follows agreed criteria. |

    | Control change | Project manager and change authority | Analysis and decision are distinct. |

    | Close and transition | Sponsor, receiving owner, and project manager | Closure, readiness, and benefits ownership are separate decisions. |

    Project Manager

    Integrates project work, information, decisions, and stakeholders within delegated authority.

    Responsibilities

    • Coordinate planning and delivery
    • Integrate performance information
    • Route decisions and escalation

    Accountabilities

    • Project integration
    • Decision traceability
    • Escalation quality
    Authority
    Makes project decisions within the charter, plan, and delegated thresholds; routes reserved decisions.
    Boundary
    Is not automatically the product owner, technical authority, acceptance authority, or sponsor.
    Delivery context
    More formally coordinating in predictive settings; often an integration and governance bridge in hybrid settings; may be absent or differently titled in adaptive product delivery.

    Delivery Team

    Produces and integrates project or product results.

    Responsibilities

    • Plan assigned work
    • Create and verify results
    • Surface impediments and evidence

    Accountabilities

    • Completed work
    • Transparent progress
    • Quality within assigned controls
    Authority
    Chooses working methods and makes technical or delivery decisions within delegated boundaries.
    Boundary
    Does not automatically own product priority, formal acceptance, or reserved governance decisions.
    Delivery context
    Generic cross-approach term; use Scrum accountabilities when specifically describing Scrum.

    See participation across the official Scrum events →
    See metric use and authority boundaries →

    Evidence status: atlas synthesis

    Functional or Resource Manager

    Provides functional capability, people, and discipline oversight.

    Responsibilities

    • Assign or negotiate resources
    • Develop capability
    • Resolve functional constraints

    Accountabilities

    • Resource availability
    • Functional capability
    • People decisions
    Authority
    Controls resources and functional decisions to the extent assigned by the organization.
    Boundary
    Does not own integrated project outcomes unless separately assigned.
    Delivery context
    Common in matrix and predictive organizations and may support adaptive teams.

    Evidence status: atlas synthesis

    Subject-Matter Expert

    Supplies specialized knowledge for analysis, design, estimates, and decisions.

    Responsibilities

    • Provide expert analysis
    • Review specialist content
    • Identify constraints

    Accountabilities

    • Sound specialist input
    • Transparent assumptions
    Authority
    Advises unless a specific technical or specialist approval right is delegated.
    Boundary
    Expertise alone does not create project, product, or acceptance authority.
    Delivery context
    Applies across delivery approaches.

    Evidence status: atlas synthesis

    Risk Owner

    Monitors an assigned risk and ensures an agreed response is carried out.

    Responsibilities

    • Monitor triggers
    • Coordinate responses
    • Escalate threshold breaches

    Accountabilities

    • Risk visibility
    • Response execution
    • Escalation
    Authority
    Acts within the approved response and delegated risk thresholds.
    Boundary
    Does not unilaterally approve baseline or funding changes.
    Delivery context
    Applies across approaches with different review cadences.

    Evidence status: atlas synthesis

    Issue or Impediment Owner

    Drives resolution of a current problem or blocker.

    Responsibilities

    • Define actions
    • Track progress
    • Escalate stalled or out-of-authority resolution

    Accountabilities

    • Visible status
    • Timely action
    • Escalation
    Authority
    Coordinates resolution within assigned limits.
    Boundary
    Assignment does not confer authority to alter controlled commitments.
    Delivery context
    In Scrum, impediment removal may be enabled by the Scrum Master while ownership remains with the person able to resolve it.

    See participation across the official Scrum events →
    See metric use and authority boundaries →

    Evidence status: atlas synthesis

    Stakeholder

    Provides needs, constraints, feedback, influence, or affected-party perspective.

    Responsibilities

    • Provide timely input
    • Review relevant information
    • Surface impacts

    Accountabilities

    • Constructive participation within the engagement model
    Authority
    Varies; stakeholder status alone does not grant approval authority.
    Boundary
    A request is not an authorized change solely because it comes from an influential stakeholder.
    Delivery context
    Engagement cadence varies across approaches.

    Evidence status: atlas synthesis

    Project Team Member

    Performs assigned project work and contributes expertise and evidence.

    Responsibilities

    • Complete work
    • Report status and risks
    • Collaborate on decisions

    Accountabilities

    • Professional delivery
    • Transparency
    • Quality
    Authority
    Makes work decisions within assigned responsibilities and team agreements.
    Boundary
    Generic predictive or hybrid role label; do not substitute it for the three Scrum accountabilities when explaining Scrum.
    Delivery context
    Common predictive and hybrid term.

    Evidence status: atlas synthesis

    Business Analyst

    Elicits, analyzes, and traces needs and requirements.

    Responsibilities

    • Elicit needs
    • Maintain requirement traceability
    • Support scope and acceptance clarity

    Accountabilities

    • Requirement quality
    • Traceability
    • Shared understanding
    Authority
    Recommends and maintains requirement information within delegated product or project governance.
    Boundary
    Does not automatically own product priority or formal acceptance.
    Delivery context
    May support predictive requirements baselines, adaptive backlog refinement, or both in hybrid work.

    Evidence status: atlas synthesis
