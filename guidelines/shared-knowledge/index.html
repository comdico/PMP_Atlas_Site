    AI Semantic Reference

    Semantic contract for AI agents, retrieval systems, generators, validators, and advanced readers.

    !!! info "Purpose"

    This page defines the preferred terminology, conceptual boundaries, ownership rules, precedence, applicability, and verification status used throughout The Project Management Knowledge Atlas. It is designed primarily for AI agents and retrieval systems, while remaining readable to advanced human users. It is not a narrative PMP study chapter.

    The Atlas is an independent study resource. Source status must be respected, and an Atlas synthesis must not be presented as official PMI guidance. Linked canonical pages contain the full reader-facing explanation. For operational role assignments, use the Roles Reference and its 49-process role map; this semantic contract remains concise.

    3. PRE-003: The Roles registry owns public role definitions; the Process Role Map owns contextual process assignments and authority boundaries.
    4. PRE-004: Artifact definitions own structure and state; the Artifact Role Map owns contextual creation, custody, review, approval, verification, acceptance, and receipt assignments.
    5. PRE-005: Lifecycle definitions own sequence, handoffs, and lifecycle context.
    6. PRE-006: Decision Guides own scenario-specific action and escalation paths.
    7. PRE-007: Shared semantic records define cross-cutting terminology and interpretation.
    8. PRE-008: Current verified source material has precedence over Atlas synthesis.
    9. PRE-009: Source-limited or synthesized material must be labeled as such.
    10. PRE-010: When authority varies by organization, do not invent a universal rule.

    AI usage guidance

    Recommended behavior

    • Follow canonical links for full explanations.
    • Preserve verification labels.
    • Distinguish official source material from Atlas synthesis.
    • State uncertainty when a record is source-limited.
    • Do not infer that missing applicability means prohibition.
    • Do not treat commercial templates as PMI-prescribed forms.
    • Do not treat process groups as sequential lifecycle phases.
    • Do not equate waterfall with every form of predictive delivery.
    • Do not equate Scrum or Kanban with all adaptive delivery.

    Avoid

    • Inventing authority.
    • Merging risk, issue, and impediment.
    • Stating earned value as merely a percentage.
    • Placing management reserve inside BAC.
    • Describing all hybrid work as a separate standardized framework.
    • Presenting Atlas-generated crosswalks as official PMI mappings.

    Roles and authority

    Sponsor

    Source-limited SEM-ROL-SPONSOR

    Champions the initiative and connects it to authorization, funding, strategic direction, and escalation authority.

    Scope: Commonly associated with authorization, strategic alignment, funding sponsorship, and decisions beyond delegated project authority.

    Aliases: project sponsor

    Distinctions:

    • The sponsor is not automatically the customer, acceptance authority, operational owner, or benefits owner.
    • Specific approval rights come from the organization's governance model.

    Does not mean: Universal owner of every project decision

    Applicability: organization specific, common across approaches

    Interpretation note: Exact sponsor authority varies by organization and initiative governance.

    Canonical public pages: Open reference

    Related processes: Develop Project Charter (PRC-DEVELOP-PROJECT-CHARTER); Perform Integrated Change Control (PRC-PERFORM-INTEGRATED-CHANGE-CONTROL); Close Project or Phase (PRC-CLOSE-PROJECT-OR-PHASE)

    Related artifacts: Project Charter (ART-CHR-001); Benefits Management Information (ART-BEN-001); Formal Closure Record (ART-CLS-002)

    Related lifecycle chapters: Conception and Authorization (LFC-001); Monitoring and Change (LFC-008); Verification, Acceptance, Transition, and Closure (LFC-010)

    Related Decision Guides: A Stakeholder Requests a Baseline Change (DEC-CHG-001); Transition or Closure Is Requested with Unresolved Obligations (DEC-CLS-001); Expected Benefits Are Not Yet Realized at Project Closure (DEC-BEN-001)

    AI guidance:

    • Use when: Identifying authorization, strategic direction, funding sponsorship, or an escalation destination.
    • Avoid: Do not assign the sponsor every approval or post-project ownership duty.
    • Answer pattern: State the commonly associated purpose, then qualify the exact authority as governance-dependent.

    Project manager

    Source-limited SEM-ROL-PROJECT-MANAGER

    Integrates project work, information, and decisions within delegated authority.

    Scope: Commonly coordinates planning, execution, monitoring, communication, and escalation across the project system.

    Aliases: PM

    Distinctions:

    • Integration responsibility does not automatically grant specialist, sponsor, procurement, compliance, or acceptance authority.
    • Matters outside delegated limits are escalated through the governing route.

    Does not mean: Universal approver; Sole producer of project work

    Applicability: common across approaches, organization specific

    Interpretation note: The extent of project-manager decision rights depends on delegated governance.

    Canonical public pages: Open reference

    Related processes: Develop Project Management Plan (PRC-DEVELOP-PROJECT-MANAGEMENT-PLAN); Direct and Manage Project Work (PRC-DIRECT-AND-MANAGE-PROJECT-WORK); Monitor and Control Project Work (PRC-MONITOR-AND-CONTROL-PROJECT-WORK)

    Related artifacts: Project Management Plan (ART-PLN-001); Work Performance Reports (ART-WPR-001); Issue Log (ART-ISS-001)

    Related lifecycle chapters: Integrated Planning (LFC-002); Execution and Team Leadership (LFC-007); Monitoring and Change (LFC-008)

    Related Decision Guides: A Stakeholder Requests a Baseline Change (DEC-CHG-001); A Risk Occurs or an Impediment Exceeds Team Authority (DEC-ISS-001); Transition or Closure Is Requested with Unresolved Obligations (DEC-CLS-001)

    AI guidance:

    • Use when: Explaining integration, coordination, analysis, routing, and escalation.
    • Avoid: Do not infer unrestricted authority from the role title.
    • Answer pattern: Describe integration responsibility and name the authority boundary relevant to the scenario.

    Project team

    Source-limited SEM-ROL-PROJECT-TEAM

    Performs and coordinates project work, contributes estimates and evidence, and resolves matters within team authority.

    Scope: Commonly owns execution details, collaboration, learning, and evidence produced by the work.

    Aliases: team, delivery team

    Distinctions:

    • Self-management may shape how work is performed without removing external governance or acceptance limits.

    Does not mean: Freedom from governance; Automatic formal acceptance authority

    Applicability: common across approaches, organization specific

    Interpretation note: Team composition and decision rights are tailored to the delivery and governance model.

    Canonical public pages: Open reference

    Related processes: Direct and Manage Project Work (PRC-DIRECT-AND-MANAGE-PROJECT-WORK); Develop Team (PRC-DEVELOP-TEAM); Manage Team (PRC-MANAGE-TEAM)

    Related artifacts: Deliverables (ART-DEL-001); Lessons Learned Register (ART-INT-002); Impediment Record (ART-ISS-002)

    Related lifecycle chapters: Execution and Team Leadership (LFC-007); Adaptive and Hybrid Delivery (LFC-009)

    Related Decision Guides: A Deliverable Fails Quality Inspection (DEC-QLT-001); A Risk Occurs or an Impediment Exceeds Team Authority (DEC-ISS-001)

    AI guidance:

    • Use when: Explaining who performs work, estimates, collaborates, or removes team-level constraints.
    • Avoid: Do not treat self-management as unlimited organizational authority.
    • Answer pattern: State the team's work purpose and separate team authority from external decision rights.

    Product owner

    Source-limited SEM-ROL-PRODUCT-OWNER

    In product-oriented adaptive contexts, orders value-focused work and makes product decisions within the governing model.

    Scope: Most precise as a Scrum accountability; other adaptive approaches may use different product roles.

    Aliases: PO

    Distinctions:

    • Product prioritization is separate from technical verification and formal acceptance unless governance explicitly combines them.

    Does not mean: Generic name for every adaptive leader; Automatic formal acceptance authority

    Applicability: scrum specific, adaptive, hybrid, organization specific

    Interpretation note: Product-role naming and authority vary outside Scrum and across organizations.

    Canonical public pages: Open reference

    Related processes: Direct and Manage Project Work (PRC-DIRECT-AND-MANAGE-PROJECT-WORK); Manage Stakeholder Engagement (PRC-MANAGE-STAKEHOLDER-ENGAGEMENT)

    Related artifacts: Requirements Documentation (ART-REQ-001); Deliverables (ART-DEL-001); Impediment Record (ART-ISS-002)

    Related lifecycle chapters: Execution and Team Leadership (LFC-007); Adaptive and Hybrid Delivery (LFC-009)

    Related Decision Guides: A Risk Occurs or an Impediment Exceeds Team Authority (DEC-ISS-001); A Verified Deliverable Is Rejected During Acceptance (DEC-ACC-001)

    AI guidance:

    • Use when: A Scrum or product-oriented context explicitly assigns value ordering or product decisions.
    • Avoid: Do not generalize the accountability to all adaptive delivery or formal acceptance.
    • Answer pattern: Name the framework or governance context before describing product-owner authority.

    Customer or acceptance authority

    Source-limited SEM-ROL-ACCEPTANCE-AUTHORITY

    Formally accepts verified deliverables or scope results under the applicable agreement or governance model.

    Scope: Commonly associated with the decision that completed, verified results satisfy agreed acceptance criteria.

    Aliases: customer, acceptance authority, accepting authority

    Distinctions:

    • Technical verification and formal acceptance are separate states and decisions.

    Does not mean: Quality inspector; Automatic sponsor or product owner

    Applicability: common across approaches, organization specific

    Interpretation note: The accepting party and evidence required depend on governance and agreements.

    Canonical public pages: Open reference

    Related processes: Validate Scope (PRC-VALIDATE-SCOPE); Control Quality (PRC-CONTROL-QUALITY); Close Project or Phase (PRC-CLOSE-PROJECT-OR-PHASE)

    Related artifacts: Verified Deliverables (ART-QLT-004); Accepted Deliverables (ART-QLT-005); Formal Closure Record (ART-CLS-002)

    Related lifecycle chapters: Quality (LFC-005); Verification, Acceptance, Transition, and Closure (LFC-010)

    Related Decision Guides: A Verified Deliverable Is Rejected During Acceptance (DEC-ACC-001); Transition or Closure Is Requested with Unresolved Obligations (DEC-CLS-001)

    AI guidance:

    • Use when: Explaining formal acceptance after technical verification.
    • Avoid: Do not collapse verification into acceptance or invent the accepting role.
    • Answer pattern: Identify the verified state, acceptance criteria, and governance-designated decision maker.

    Governance body

    Source-limited SEM-ROL-GOVERNANCE-BODY

    Provides organizational direction and decisions under the initiative's governance model.

    Scope: May decide funding, continuation, exceptions, baselines, escalation, or closure when those rights are explicitly assigned.

    Aliases: steering committee, governing body

    Distinctions:

    • Its mandate is defined by organizational governance rather than by a universal Atlas rule.

    Does not mean: A required committee with identical powers in every organization

    Applicability: organization specific, common across approaches

    Interpretation note: Governance structures and mandates are organization-specific.

    Canonical public pages: Open reference

    Related processes: Develop Project Charter (PRC-DEVELOP-PROJECT-CHARTER); Perform Integrated Change Control (PRC-PERFORM-INTEGRATED-CHANGE-CONTROL); Close Project or Phase (PRC-CLOSE-PROJECT-OR-PHASE)

    Related artifacts: Project Charter (ART-CHR-001); Project Management Plan (ART-PLN-001); Formal Closure Record (ART-CLS-002)

    Related lifecycle chapters: Conception and Authorization (LFC-001); Monitoring and Change (LFC-008); Verification, Acceptance, Transition, and Closure (LFC-010)

    Related Decision Guides: A Stakeholder Requests a Baseline Change (DEC-CHG-001); A Compliance Problem Is Discovered (DEC-CMP-001); Transition or Closure Is Requested with Unresolved Obligations (DEC-CLS-001)

    AI guidance:

    • Use when: A decision exceeds delegated project, team, or specialist authority.
    • Avoid: Do not assume a committee exists or assign it unstated powers.
    • Answer pattern: Refer to the designated governance route and qualify its authority as organization-specific.

    Change-control authority or CCB

    Source-limited SEM-ROL-CHANGE-AUTHORITY

    Evaluates and disposes controlled changes within an approved mandate.

    Scope: Commonly decides changes that affect controlled commitments, baselines, or other governance thresholds.

    Aliases: change authority, change control board, CCB

    Distinctions:

    • A standing CCB is one governance design; change authority may be assigned differently.

    Does not mean: Required review body for every adaptive reprioritization

    Applicability: predictive, hybrid, organization specific

    Interpretation note: Thresholds and decision rights are defined by the governing change-control model.

    Canonical public pages: Open reference

    Related processes: Monitor and Control Project Work (PRC-MONITOR-AND-CONTROL-PROJECT-WORK); Perform Integrated Change Control (PRC-PERFORM-INTEGRATED-CHANGE-CONTROL)

    Related artifacts: Change Requests (ART-CHG-001); Change Log (ART-CHG-004); Project Management Plan (ART-PLN-001)

    Related lifecycle chapters: Monitoring and Change (LFC-008); Adaptive and Hybrid Delivery (LFC-009)

    Related Decision Guides: A Stakeholder Requests a Baseline Change (DEC-CHG-001); A Compliance Problem Is Discovered (DEC-CMP-001)

    AI guidance:

    • Use when: A proposed action may alter a controlled baseline or commitment.
    • Avoid: Do not require CCB review for every work-level adjustment.
    • Answer pattern: Identify the controlled commitment, threshold, and designated change authority.

    Operational owner

    Source-limited SEM-ROL-OPERATIONAL-OWNER

    Accepts operational responsibility for transferred outputs, support, controls, and residual obligations.

    Scope: Commonly associated with transition readiness and continuing operation after handoff.

    Aliases: operations owner, receiving owner

    Distinctions:

    • Operational ownership is distinct from formal project closure and benefits ownership.

    Does not mean: Universal post-project benefits owner

    Applicability: organization specific, common across approaches

    Interpretation note: The receiving organization and its authority are organization-specific.

    Canonical public pages: Open reference

    Related processes: Close Project or Phase (PRC-CLOSE-PROJECT-OR-PHASE)

    AI guidance:

    • Use when: Explaining transition, operational readiness, or post-transfer support responsibility.
    • Avoid: Do not assume operations also owns every intended benefit.
    • Answer pattern: Name the receiving role, transferred responsibilities, evidence, and unresolved obligations.

    Aliases: benefit owner

    Distinctions:

    Does not mean: A universal authority model prescribed by the Atlas

    Distinctions:

    Delivery terminology

    Stakeholder Engagement Assessment Matrix

    Verified SEM-STK-ENGAGEMENT-ASSESSMENT-MATRIX

    A working assessment that compares each stakeholder identifier's current and desired engagement state so the team can identify gaps and tailor engagement actions.

    Scope: The matrix uses Unaware, Resistant, Neutral, Supportive, and Leading states with C for current, D for desired, and C/D when the states are the same; identifiers should replace personal names where assessment information is sensitive.

    Aliases: stakeholder engagement assessment, current-and-desired engagement matrix

    Distinctions:

    • The matrix assesses engagement state; the Stakeholder Register owns stakeholder identity, influence, authority, and broader profile information.
    • The matrix informs the Stakeholder Engagement Plan but is not the plan itself.
    • The matrix may inform communication choices but is not the Communications Management Plan.
    • A power-interest or similar stakeholder grid classifies influence or interest; it does not show current-versus-desired engagement.
    • The matrix is a working assessment tool and is not asserted as a formal process output.

    Does not mean: Stakeholder Register; Stakeholder Engagement Plan; Communications Management Plan; Power-interest grid; Formal process output

    Applicability: predictive, adaptive, hybrid, context specific

    Interpretation note: Lesson 3 supports the five-state current-versus-desired model; the extended evidence, action, owner, and review fields are clearly identified as an Atlas working-resource design.

    Canonical public pages: Open reference

    Related processes: Identify Stakeholders (PRC-IDENTIFY-STAKEHOLDERS); Plan Stakeholder Engagement (PRC-PLAN-STAKEHOLDER-ENGAGEMENT); Manage Stakeholder Engagement (PRC-MANAGE-STAKEHOLDER-ENGAGEMENT); Monitor Stakeholder Engagement (PRC-MONITOR-STAKEHOLDER-ENGAGEMENT); Plan Communications Management (PRC-PLAN-COMMUNICATIONS-MANAGEMENT); Manage Communications (PRC-MANAGE-COMMUNICATIONS); Monitor Communications (PRC-MONITOR-COMMUNICATIONS)

    Related artifacts: Stakeholder Register (ART-STK-001); Stakeholder Engagement Plan (ART-STK-002); Communications Management Plan (ART-COM-001); Issue Log (ART-ISS-001); Risk Register (ART-RSK-001); Lessons Learned Register (ART-INT-002); Change Requests (ART-CHG-001); Stakeholder Engagement Assessment Matrix (ART-STK-003)

    Related lifecycle chapters: Integrated Planning (LFC-002); Execution and Team Leadership (LFC-007); Monitoring and Change (LFC-008); Adaptive and Hybrid Delivery (LFC-009)

    AI guidance:

    • Use when: Comparing observed stakeholder engagement with the engagement needed for project success or explaining the resulting engagement gap.
    • Avoid: Do not expose sensitive personal assessments, substitute the matrix for its related registers or plans, or present it as a required formal output in every delivery approach.
    • Answer pattern: Define the five states and C/D markers, explain the gap, then distinguish the matrix from the register, engagement plan, communications plan, and influence-interest grids.

    Predictive

    Atlas synthesis SEM-DEL-PREDICTIVE

    A delivery approach that establishes a comparatively broad authorized plan and baselines when work can be usefully defined in advance.

    Scope: Planning and control are tailored; prediction does not eliminate iteration, learning, or change.

    Aliases: predictive delivery

    Distinctions:

    • Predictive is a delivery approach
    • not a claim that the future is perfectly known.

    Does not mean: No iteration; No change

    Applicability: predictive

    Interpretation note: The concise contrast is an Atlas synthesis of the registered delivery-approach source set.

    Canonical public pages: Open reference

    Related processes: Develop Project Management Plan (PRC-DEVELOP-PROJECT-MANAGEMENT-PLAN); Perform Integrated Change Control (PRC-PERFORM-INTEGRATED-CHANGE-CONTROL)

    Related artifacts: Project Management Plan (ART-PLN-001); Scope Baseline (ART-SCP-001); Schedule Baseline (ART-SCH-005); Cost Baseline (ART-CST-002)

    Related lifecycle chapters: Integrated Planning (LFC-002); Adaptive and Hybrid Delivery (LFC-009)

    AI guidance:

    • Use when: Work can be planned comparatively broadly and governed through authorized plans or baselines.
    • Avoid: Do not describe predictive delivery as non-iterative or immune to change.

    Adaptive

    Atlas synthesis SEM-DEL-ADAPTIVE

    A delivery approach using short planning and delivery horizons with frequent review when learning and change are expected.

    Scope: Includes multiple frameworks and methods; form, cadence, roles, and artifacts vary.

    Aliases: adaptive delivery

    Distinctions:

    • Adaptive is broader than Scrum or Kanban.

    Does not mean: Scrum only; Kanban only; No governance

    Applicability: adaptive

    Interpretation note: Cross-approach process applicability is Atlas synthesis, not an official PMI agile crosswalk.

    Canonical public pages: Open reference

    Related processes: Collect Requirements (PRC-COLLECT-REQUIREMENTS); Direct and Manage Project Work (PRC-DIRECT-AND-MANAGE-PROJECT-WORK); Monitor and Control Project Work (PRC-MONITOR-AND-CONTROL-PROJECT-WORK)

    Related lifecycle chapters: Adaptive and Hybrid Delivery (LFC-009)

    AI guidance:

    • Use when: Learning, feedback, and changing priorities call for short planning and delivery horizons.
    • Avoid: Do not infer one mandatory framework or remove governance constraints.
    • Answer pattern: Describe the feedback cadence and tailored roles, artifacts, and controls.

    Does not mean: All adaptive work; All agile work

    Interpretation note: Framework-specific mechanics should be verified against an applicable Scrum source before being asserted.

    Canonical public pages: Open reference

    Related lifecycle chapters: Adaptive and Hybrid Delivery (LFC-009)

    Velocity

    Verified SEM-MET-VELOCITY

    Aliases: iteration velocity, Sprint velocity

    Distinctions:

    • Only Done work counts.
    • Team-specific story points are not hours or comparable across teams.

    Does not mean: Productivity; Partial-completion credit

    Applicability: adaptive, scrum specific, hybrid, context specific

    Canonical public pages: Open reference

    AI guidance:

    • Use when: Same-team iteration forecasting is in scope.
    • Avoid: Never compare teams or count unfinished work.
    • Answer pattern: Define the team and iteration and link to the canonical metric page.

    Lead time

    Verified SEM-MET-LEAD-TIME

    Elapsed time from a defined request, order, or commitment point until delivery.

    Scope: The selected start and finish points must be stated; the Adaptive Metrics registry separately owns customer, commitment, procurement, and schedule-lead distinctions.

    Aliases: customer lead time, commitment lead time

    Distinctions:

    • May include waiting before active work.
    • It is at least cycle time only when the finish is shared and its start is earlier.
    • Predictive schedule lead is a precedence adjustment rather than this elapsed metric.

    Does not mean: Cycle time unless endpoints match; Schedule lead

    Applicability: adaptive, hybrid, context specific

    Canonical public pages: Open reference

    AI guidance:

    • Use when: Customer commitment or procurement responsiveness is discussed.
    • Avoid: Do not leave boundaries implicit or confuse elapsed lead time with schedule lead.
    • Answer pattern: State start finish and time convention.

    Closure and benefits handoff

    Closure and benefits handoff

    Source-limited SEM-CLS-BENEFITS-HANDOFF

    The explicit separation and connection among formal acceptance, transition, formal project closure, operational ownership, benefits ownership, and continuing benefits measurement.

    Scope: Formal acceptance confirms agreed results; transition transfers readiness and responsibility; formal closure completes project or phase obligations; operational and benefits owners continue assigned post-project responsibilities.

    Aliases: benefits transition, post-project benefits handoff

    Distinctions:

    • Formal project closure may occur before all benefits are realized.
    • Continuing measurement requires an explicit handoff.
    • Operational ownership and benefits ownership may be assigned to different roles.
    • The Atlas does not assert a universal benefits-authority model.

    Does not mean: All benefits must be realized before closure; The project manager owns benefits indefinitely

    Applicability: organization specific, common across approaches

    Interpretation note: Post-project operational and benefits authority depends on the receiving organization.

    Canonical public pages: Open reference

    Related processes: Validate Scope (PRC-VALIDATE-SCOPE); Close Project or Phase (PRC-CLOSE-PROJECT-OR-PHASE)

    Related artifacts: Accepted Deliverables (ART-QLT-005); Transition Package (ART-TRN-001); Benefits Management Information (ART-BEN-001); Benefits Handoff Record (ART-BEN-002); Formal Closure Record (ART-CLS-002)

    Related lifecycle chapters: Verification, Acceptance, Transition, and Closure (LFC-010)

    Related Decision Guides: Transition or Closure Is Requested with Unresolved Obligations (DEC-CLS-001); Expected Benefits Are Not Yet Realized at Project Closure (DEC-BEN-001)

    AI guidance:

    • Use when: Explaining acceptance, transition, closure, or responsibility after project completion.
    • Avoid: Do not delay closure until every benefit occurs or assign universal post-project ownership.
    • Answer pattern: Separate each state and name the explicit operational and benefits handoffs.
