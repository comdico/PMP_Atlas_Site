    Requirements and Scope

    Needs become controllable when they can be traced through scope, decomposition, delivery, verification, and formal acceptance.

    From a need to controlled scope

    A requirement states a capability or condition that a result must satisfy. A requirement may come from a customer, user, law, contract, operational need, technical constraint, or business objective. Collecting requirements is not simply writing down requests; the team must clarify meaning, resolve conflicts, test feasibility, and record how each important requirement will be evaluated.

    Product scope describes the features and functions of the product, service, or result. Project scope describes the work required to create that result. For ServiceBridge, “support accessible appointment scheduling” is product scope. Designing, configuring, testing, training, migrating data, and transitioning the service are parts of project scope.

    The Project Scope Statement establishes the detailed boundary developed during planning. The Scope Baseline adds the approved Work Breakdown Structure and WBS dictionary so the boundary can be planned and controlled.

    WBS terms in plain language

    A Work Breakdown Structure, or WBS, organizes the complete project scope into a hierarchy. The team decomposes large deliverables into smaller components until the lowest planned components can be estimated, assigned, monitored, and controlled.

    A work package is the lowest WBS component for which cost and duration can be estimated and managed. It is not the same as an activity. A work package describes a piece of scope; activities describe the actions needed to complete it.

    A control account is a management control point where scope, schedule, and cost information are integrated and compared with performance. A planning package is known work below a control account that has not yet been decomposed into detailed work packages. It allows later detail without pretending the team knows more than it does.

    The WBS dictionary explains each component’s boundary, deliverables, assumptions, responsibilities, milestones, and other planning information. Without the dictionary, short WBS labels can be interpreted differently by different people.

    Traceability, verification, and acceptance

    The Requirements Traceability Matrix connects a requirement to its origin, business reason, approved scope, design or delivery work, test evidence, acceptance evidence, owner, and status. This connection helps the team detect omitted requirements and evaluate the effect of a requested change.

    Completion does not move directly to customer acceptance. Control Quality first checks whether a completed deliverable conforms to requirements and quality criteria. Conforming work becomes a Verified Deliverable. Validate Scope then presents that verified work to the authorized customer or acceptance role. Approval creates an Accepted Deliverable.

    In predictive work, the team may approve a broad scope baseline before delivery. Adaptive work develops detailed scope through a Product Backlog and short planning horizons. A backlog item’s acceptance criteria describe the conditions for that item; the Definition of Done is the shared quality commitment for an increment. Neither automatically replaces organizational authorization or formal acceptance. Hybrid work links evolving backlog items to the controlled scope, compliance, contract, and acceptance obligations that remain fixed.

    For the exam, remember: requirements describe needs, the scope statement defines the detailed boundary, the WBS decomposes project scope, activities describe scheduled work, quality control verifies, and the authorized stakeholder accepts.

    Local sequence

    1. Plan how requirements and scope will be developed, validated, and controlled.
    2. Elicit and analyze needs into Requirements Documentation.
    3. Connect requirements to origin and downstream evidence in the Requirements Traceability Matrix.
    4. Define product and project scope boundaries.
    5. Decompose approved scope into the WBS and WBS dictionary within the Scope Baseline.
    6. Deliver and control scope while preserving traceability.
    7. Verify completed deliverables before seeking formal acceptance.

    Handoffs

    What enters

    Charter, stakeholder needs, product information, assumptions, constraints, standards, risk, and delivery approach.

    What this work accomplishes

    Defines what must be satisfied, what work is authorized, and what evidence will demonstrate completion.

    What moves next

    Approved scope drives schedule, cost, quality, resources, procurement, delivery, verification, acceptance, and change control.

    Feedback and rejection loops

    Unclear or conflicting requirements return to stakeholders for analysis. Decomposition can reveal missing scope or infeasible assumptions. During delivery, requested scope changes enter integrated change control. A deliverable that fails inspection returns for defect analysis and authorized rework; a verified deliverable that is not accepted returns for clarification, correction, change evaluation, or another authorized disposition.

    Roles, authority, and approaches

    Stakeholders supply needs and acceptance expectations. The project team analyzes and decomposes the work. The project manager coordinates control. An authorized customer, sponsor, product owner, or other designated role makes the applicable acceptance decision; verification by the quality function is not that formal acceptance.

    Predictive work may baseline a broad scope structure. Adaptive work refines prioritized items and acceptance criteria over short horizons while preserving outcome and traceability. Hybrid work must map evolving items to controlled commitments and approval boundaries.

    Artifact states and exam distinctions

    A need becomes a documented requirement, a requirement becomes traceable to approved scope and delivery work, a completed deliverable becomes verified through quality control, and a verified deliverable becomes accepted only through an authorized acceptance decision. The WBS organizes scope; it is not a schedule or organizational chart.
